🎯 Practice scenarios

Not part of Google Ads — your interview training ground
💡 How to use this page: read the interviewer prompt out loud, answer aloud using the real dashboard pages (don't peek), then open the model answer and compare. The green boxes are "advanced flourishes" — lines that make you sound senior. Work top to bottom; difficulty escalates.
PLAYBOOK

Primary vs. supporting metrics — your operating system for every answer

Primary metrics (lead with these, always, in this order): Cost → Conversions → Cost / conv. For lead gen, these are the business. Everything else exists to explain them.

Supporting metrics (use to diagnose, never to headline): CTR, Avg. CPC, Conv. rate, Clicks, Impressions.

  • CTR benchmark (lead-gen Search): 5–8% = doing things right · above 8% = excellent · below 5% = look at search terms, targeting, or ad copy. On this account: Kee Klamp 6.21% ✓ · KeeGuard 5.00% (borderline) · Gates 4.20% (slightly under) · Fall Protection 2.70% ✗ · PMax 0.90% (exception — Display/YouTube inventory, don't judge it by CTR).
  • Conv. rate benchmark: 5–15% = doing things right · under 5% = problem. Here: KeeGuard 8.50% ✓ · Kee Klamp 7.56% ✓ · Gates 5.83% ✓ · Fall Protection 4.12% ✗ — under the floor, and that's your diagnosis anchor.
  • Prerequisite before trusting ANY of this: conversion tracking must count real leads (form fills + calls here), not button clicks or page views. Say this early in the interview — it's the mark of someone who's been burned.
  • Timeframes: never judge on less than 7 days; routine reads at 7–30 days; significant changes on 2/4/8-week averages; always sanity-check a second timeframe before acting.
  • Blended numbers lie: account CTR reads 2.63% — but that's PMax's 208k cheap impressions dragging a blend. Segment before you diagnose. Same for the account's $105.75 CPA: it spans $55 to $192.
Sound senior: "I keep primary and supporting metrics separate. Cost per lead is the verdict; CTR and conversion rate are the evidence. If CTR is under 5% I look at search terms and copy; if conversion rate is under 5% I look at traffic quality and the landing page. And I never read either at the campaign level only — the campaign number is an average that hides the problem."
CORE 1

"Walk me through this account."

INTERVIEWER"Here's our Google Ads account. Take a look and tell me what you see."

Your task: 2 minutes max, out loud, Campaigns page only. Primary metrics first, then one supporting-metric observation, then your headline finding. Do NOT list every number.

Model answer

"Let me orient first: Campaigns view, May through July, five campaigns — four Search, one Performance Max — and the conversion action is real leads: quote form fills plus calls, so the data's trustworthy. Primary metrics: $80,690 spent, 763 leads, $105.75 per lead. Spend is flat versus the prior period but leads are down 15% and cost per lead is up 22% — so this is an efficiency problem, not a budget problem."

"Supporting metrics point at where: Kee Klamp converts at $55 with a 6.2% CTR — that's my benchmark of healthy. Fall Protection Systems is $192 a lead on a 2.7% CTR and 4.1% conversion rate — both under the floor for lead-gen Search. It also takes 40% of the spend. That's where I'd dig first."

Flourish: end with intent — "Mind if I step down into its ad groups? A campaign number is an average, and averages hide problems."
CORE 2

The step-down drill: campaign → ad group → ad → keyword → search term

INTERVIEWER"You said Fall Protection Systems looks bad. Its CTR is 2.7%. What do you do with that?"

Your task: narrate the full dissection using the real pages: Ad groups → Ads → Audiences/keywords → Search terms. Never make a decision at the level you spotted the problem.

Model answer

"2.7% is the campaign blend — I don't act on a blend. Step down to ad groups: 'Fall Protection – General' is 2.25% CTR at $296 a lead, while its siblings are 3.4% and 3.8% at ~$130. So the campaign isn't broken — one ad group is. Step down again to the ad: that group's RSA is rated 'Average' with generic copy. Then the keywords: its broad-match terms sit at Quality Score 3–5. And the search terms close the case: 'fall protection jobs near me', 'free training ppt' — $2,900 spent, zero leads."

"So the low CTR isn't one problem, it's a chain: broad match admits junk queries → ads look irrelevant to those queries → CTR falls → Quality Score falls → CPC rises. Fix at the entry point: match types and negatives first, then copy."

Flourish: "The rule I work by: diagnose top-down, fix bottom-up. You spot it at the campaign level, you prove it at the search-term level, and you fix it there first."
CORE 3

"Leads dropped from 300 to 200. Investigate."

INTERVIEWER"Leadership is asking why lead volume fell by a third over the quarter. Walk me through how you'd figure it out."

Your task: the flagship question. Framework out loud: measurement → segment → volume-vs-efficiency → external. End at Change history. (Full script in the 🎓 coach, "The lead drop" tab.)

Model answer (compressed)
  1. Verify it's real: conversion tag, site/form changes, does the CRM see the same decline? Only real leads count — if someone added a button-click conversion, the whole analysis is garbage.
  2. Segment the −99: by campaign it's FPS ≈ −35 (starts week of Jun 14), KeeGuard ≈ −20 (budget-capped, 38% lost IS), Gates ≈ −15 (CPC inflation), PMax ≈ −10 (stale creative).
  3. Volume vs. efficiency: spend flat, clicks slightly up, conversion rate down → buying more but worse traffic. Efficiency problem.
  4. Confirm mechanism in Change history: Jun 14, 9:12 AM — 247 keywords to broad; 9:17 AM — bid strategy to Maximize conversions with no CPA guardrail. Decline starts that week. Correlation plus mechanism.
Flourish: "And I'd check one boring thing everyone skips: the timeframe. This is a 3-month view — I'd re-cut it as four 2-week windows to see whether the decline is a step (a change did it) or a slope (the market did it). Here it's a step, dated Jun 14."
ADVANCED 4

"Same budget. Get me more leads."

INTERVIEWER"You can't have another dollar. How do you grow lead volume next month?"

Your task: reallocation with arithmetic, out loud. Use Budget/day and Cost/conv. columns; the money is misallocated, not insufficient.

Model answer

"The account spends ~$890/day. $360 of it goes to a $192-per-lead campaign while a $73-per-lead campaign is capped at $220 and losing 38% of its impressions to budget. I'd move ~$100/day from Fall Protection to KeeGuard. Rough math: $100/day ≈ $3,000/month. At KeeGuard's ~$75–85 marginal cost per lead — I assume the marginal lead costs more than the average one — that's roughly 35–40 extra leads. Fall Protection loses maybe 15 of its expensive leads. Net: +20–25 leads/month at a lower blended CPA, before I've fixed anything else."

"Then the free wins: negatives on the junk search terms (that's ~$950/month spent on zero-conversion queries), and reverting the worst broad keywords to phrase. That's recovery without a dollar of new budget."

Flourish: saying "marginal cost per lead, not average" — and knowing lost-IS-budget means guaranteed cheap volume exists — is what separates senior media buyers from dashboard readers.
ADVANCED 5

"Our Google rep says move everything to broad match + Smart Bidding."

INTERVIEWER"We got a call from our Google account rep recommending we switch all campaigns to broad match with automated bidding. Should we?"

Your task: disagree diplomatically, with evidence from this account. This tests judgment and vendor skepticism.

Model answer

"We ran that exact play on June 14 in Fall Protection Systems, and we have the receipts: CTR fell from ~4.5% to 2.7%, conversion rate under 5%, cost per lead nearly doubled to $192. Broad match plus Smart Bidding can work — but only with guardrails this account didn't have: a target CPA (there was none — pure Maximize conversions), a mature negative list (there were 3 negatives, added six weeks late), and enough conversion volume per campaign for the algorithm to learn on."

"So my answer is: not account-wide, not untested. If we try it again, it goes through Experiments as a 50/50 split with a tCPA guardrail, judged at 95% confidence on cost per lead — exactly like the landing-page test that's running now. Google's recommendations optimize for Google's coverage; our job is to adopt the ones that survive our own testing."

Flourish: "Auto-apply recommendations is turned off in every account I run. Optimization score is a coverage metric, not a performance metric — I treat 81.2% as a to-review list, not a to-do list."
ADVANCED 6

"CPCs are up 11% — a new competitor entered. Respond."

INTERVIEWER"Auction insights shows bluewatermfg.com bidding on our terms and CPCs climbing. What's your move?"

Your task: Insights page → Auction insights card. Avoid the trap answer ("bid more").

Model answer

"First, I don't start a bidding war — that's paying Google to fight. Three moves instead: (1) Win on Quality Score, not bid: tighter ad groups and copy that mirrors the query cut real CPC; Gates & Walkways at $8.45 CPC and QS 7–8 has room. (2) Defend the brand: exact-match 'kee klamp' and 'keeguard' terms at QS 10 are cheap to hold and expensive for them to attack — I'd watch whether they bid on our brand and add brand campaigns if needed. (3) Compete where they aren't: our lost impression share on KeeGuard is budget-driven, 38% — I can buy guaranteed cheap impressions there while they overpay on the head terms."

Flourish: "I'd also check their angle — if they're winning overlap share with a lower price point, that's a message-and-landing-page problem, and no bid changes fix a message problem."
EXPERT 7

"Sales says the leads are junk."

INTERVIEWER"Volume looks fine but our sales team says half the leads from ads go nowhere. What do you do?"

Your task: the measurement-maturity question. Primary vs. secondary conversions, offline import, value-based bidding.

Model answer

"This account optimizes to form fills and calls — that's the right primary conversion, but it's the top of the funnel. Google can't tell a $200k KeeGuard project inquiry from a student downloading a spec sheet; Conv. value is $0 across the board. So step one is closing the loop: import offline conversion stages from the CRM — MQL, SQL, closed-won — matched by GCLID. Qualified stages become the new primary conversions we bid to; raw form fills move to secondary — tracked, visible, but not optimized toward."

"Then I'd find where junk concentrates. My bet from this account: the broad-match traffic in Fall Protection – General and PMax's form fills — PMax will happily manufacture cheap low-quality leads if you let it optimize on form submissions. With value data flowing, I can switch those campaigns to tROAS or value-weighted tCPA, and 'volume' conversations become 'pipeline' conversations."

Flourish: "Cost per lead is a proxy metric. The real KPI is cost per qualified opportunity — and the fastest way to look senior in B2B is to be the person who wires that up."
EXPERT 8

"We recovered to 300. Now leadership wants 400."

INTERVIEWER"Assume you've fixed the decline. Where do the next 100 leads a month come from?"

Your task: growth thinking with a sequence, not a wish list. Name where volume physically exists.

Model answer

"In order of certainty: (1) Buy back lost impression share — KeeGuard's 38% lost-to-budget is real, auctioned demand we're declining daily; fully funding it is ~+35 leads at a known CPA. (2) Expand what's proven — Kee Klamp at $55/lead scales into adjacent SKU and spec-driven queries ('galvanized pipe fittings structural', railing code searches). (3) Rebuild Fall Protection properly — phrase match, negatives, tCPA ~$90; its addressable volume is the account's biggest. (4) New demand, tested small — a competitor-terms campaign and PMax scaled only after lead-quality data exists. Each step gates the next: I scale a thing only after its CPA is proven at the current level."

Flourish: "I'd set the expectation that lead 301–400 costs more than lead 200–300 — marginal CPA rises as you buy deeper. I'd budget the 400 target at ~$110–120 blended, not today's $95, and say so up front. Under-promising there is a credibility play."
EXPERT 9

"Yesterday we only got 2 leads. The client is panicking."

INTERVIEWER"It's Tuesday morning. Monday's report shows 2 leads vs. the usual 9–10 a day. The client wants changes NOW. What do you do?"

Your task: the discipline question. Timeframe rules under pressure.

Model answer

"First: one day is not a decision-making timeframe — I don't touch bids, budgets, or keywords on 24 hours of data. Daily lead flow is lumpy, B2B especially (Mondays and Fridays swing, conversion lag means yesterday's clicks convert tomorrow). My floor for reading data is 7 days; for significant changes, 2–8 week averages."

"But 'don't panic' isn't 'do nothing.' I'd spend 15 minutes ruling out breakage, because outages ARE visible in a day: Is the campaign serving (status, budget exhausted, disapprovals)? Is the site up and the form working — submit a test lead? Is the conversion tag firing? Billing failure? If all clear, I tell the client: 'Everything's serving and tracking; this looks like normal daily variance plus conversion lag — here's the 7-day trend, and here's the date we'll reassess.' Giving them a review date is what actually stops the panic."

Flourish: "The skill isn't resisting changes — it's knowing which problems show up in a day (breakage) versus which need weeks (performance). I react instantly to the first kind and deliberately slowly to the second."
DRILLS

Solo assignments — do these before the interview

1 · Timed walkthrough. Hide hints (💡 top bar), open Campaigns, record yourself on your phone doing Core 1 in under 2 minutes. Listen back once. Primary metrics first — if you hear yourself open with CTR, redo it.
2 · Mental math reps. Cover the Cost/conv. column with your hand and compute it for 3 campaigns from Cost ÷ Conversions (e.g., $10,541 ÷ 192 ≈ $55). Do the same for one CTR (Clicks ÷ Impr.) and one conv. rate (Conv. ÷ Clicks). Interviewers notice when the math is instant.
3 · The step-down chain, aloud, 90 seconds. Campaigns → Ad groups → Ads → Search keywords → Search terms, narrating Fall Protection's problem at each level without pausing. This is Core 2 as muscle memory.
4 · Write 10 negative keywords. From the Search terms tab, draft a shared negative list (careers, free/training, DIY, informational). Bonus: say which match type each negative gets and why.
5 · Fix the weak ad. The "Average"-strength RSA in Fall Protection – General: write 8 new headlines and 3 descriptions that mirror high-intent queries ("fall protection systems for flat roofs") and exclude job-seekers by wording.
6 · Benchmark flashcards. From memory: lead-gen Search CTR good/excellent/bad thresholds; conv. rate floor and healthy range; minimum decision timeframe; the two June 14 changes and their timestamps. If you can cite "9:12 AM and 9:17 AM," you own the room.
7 · The columns tour. In a real account (or here, conceptually): Columns → Modify — know that you'd add "Search lost IS (budget)", "Search lost IS (rank)", "Conv. value/cost", and "All conv." — and be ready to say why each earns a spot while 100 others don't.

How to walk an interviewer through this account

Structure beats detail. Use this 4-step order — it shows you think top-down like a marketer, not cell-by-cell like a spreadsheet.

  1. Orient (15 sec): say what you're looking at before analyzing it.
"This is the Campaigns view for KEE Safety's account, May through July, compared to the previous 3 months. Five campaigns — four Search, one Performance Max — spending about $27k a month, with 'Leads' (quote form + calls) as the conversion action."
  1. Headline the account totals: lead with the two numbers that matter to the business.
"Top line: 763 leads at $105.75 each. Spend is roughly flat vs. the prior period, but conversions are down ~15% and cost per lead is up ~22% — so this is an efficiency problem, not a budget problem. The chart confirms it: leads have slid from ~300/month in May to ~200 in July."
  1. Contrast best vs. worst campaign: show you can rank by what matters (cost/conv., not clicks).
"Kee Klamp is my most efficient campaign — $55 a lead at a 6.2% CTR, classic high-intent product searches. Fall Protection Systems is the outlier: it takes 40% of spend but delivers leads at $192, three and a half times more expensive."
  1. Drill down with intent: Ad groups → the damage is one ad group. Audiences/keywords → the wasted search terms. Change history → the Jun 14 timeline. Then give recommendations. Every page you open should answer a question you just raised aloud.

Rules of thumb under pressure

  • Never read a metric without interpreting it ("CTR is 2.7%… which is low for Search, suggesting loose targeting").
  • Tie every observation to money or leads — that's what KEE Safety's hiring manager cares about.
  • If asked something you don't know, narrate how you'd find out ("I'd check the search terms report / Change history").
  • B2B nuance: a lead isn't revenue. Mention lead quality and offline conversion tracking — it's a differentiator.

Don't jump to an answer — walk a framework. Interviewers score the process.

Step 1 — Rule out a measurement problem first

  • Did the conversion tag break? Check Google Tag Manager / tag diagnostics and whether the website or quote form changed recently.
  • Does GA4 (or the CRM) show the same drop? If Ads dropped but the CRM didn't, it's tracking, not demand.
"Before touching campaigns, I'd verify the drop is real — check the conversion tag, recent site changes, and whether the CRM sees the same decline. A third of 'performance drops' are tracking issues."

Step 2 — Segment the drop

Break the −99 leads down by campaign, device, geography, and week. In this account:

  • Fall Protection Systems: ≈ −35/mo. Drop starts the week of Jun 14 — exactly when broad match + Maximize conversions were applied. Smoking gun in Change history; receipts in the Search terms tab.
  • KeeGuard: ≈ −20/mo. Rising CPCs mean its capped budget buys fewer clicks; lost impression share (budget) climbed to 38%.
  • Gates & Walkways: ≈ −15/mo. CPC inflation ($8.45 and rising; new competitor in Auction insights).
  • PMax: ≈ −10/mo. Creative fatigue; assets not refreshed all quarter.
  • The remainder is normal noise/seasonality.

Step 3 — Separate volume vs. efficiency

"Spend was flat, clicks actually rose slightly, but conversion rate fell — so we're buying more but worse traffic. That's an efficiency problem concentrated in the broad-match change, compounded by our best campaign being budget-starved."

Step 4 — Check external factors (mention, then de-prioritize)

  • Seasonality: construction/facilities budgets can dip mid-summer — compare year-over-year, check Google Trends.
  • Competition: Auction insights — bluewatermfg.com is new and CPCs rose ~11%.
  • Demand shift: any change in KEE's pricing, lead forms, or landing pages (the experiment changed the form for half of KeeGuard traffic — know your own tests!).

Recommendations — in priority order

Give 3–5, each with the action, the reason, and the expected impact. Quantify where possible.

  1. Fix Fall Protection Systems' match types. Audit the search terms report, add negatives (job-seeker, DIY, informational queries), move proven queries back to phrase/exact, and set a target CPA (~$90). Expected: cost/lead from $192 toward ~$100, recovering ~25–30 leads/mo without new budget.
  2. Reallocate budget to KeeGuard. Shift ~$100/day from Fall Protection to the budget-limited KeeGuard campaign (38% lost IS, $73/lead, 8.5% conv. rate). Expected: ~+30–35 leads/mo at the account's second-best CPA. Together, items 1–2 plausibly recover the 100 lost monthly leads.
  3. Close the measurement gap. Conv. value is $0 — the account can't tell a $500 Kee Klamp inquiry from a $200k KeeGuard project. Import offline conversions (MQL → SQL → closed) so bidding optimizes for revenue, not form fills. Very strong B2B talking point.
  4. Contain rising CPCs in Gates & Walkways. Improve Quality Score (tighter ad groups, copy matching query intent, faster landing pages), monitor Auction insights, apply a tCPA cap.
  5. Rehabilitate PMax before scaling it. Refresh creatives, add customer-list + site-visitor signals, exclude brand terms, gate more budget on CRM lead quality.

Close with a plan, not a list

"Week 1 I'd fix tracking and match types; week 2 reallocate budget; then set up offline conversion import. I'd expect to be back near 300 leads/month within 6–8 weeks at a blended CPA under $95 — and with lead-quality data flowing in, we could then talk about scaling, not just recovering."

Every metric on these screens, in one line each

Impr. (Impressions) How many times ads were shown. Visibility, not interest.
Clicks Times someone clicked an ad. You pay per click on Search.
CTR Clicks ÷ impressions. Relevance signal. Search benchmark ~3–6%; PMax looks much lower by design.
Avg. CPC Cost ÷ clicks. Rises with competition, falls with Quality Score.
Cost Total spend for the period.
Conversions Here: leads (quote form + calls). Always ask "conversions of what?"
Conv. rate Conversions ÷ clicks. Landing page + traffic quality. 5–15% healthy for lead-gen Search; under 5% = investigate.
Primary vs. secondary conversions Primary = what bidding optimizes toward (real leads: forms + calls). Secondary = tracked for visibility only (e.g., funnel steps, Calendly views). Never let a soft action sit as primary.
All conv. Primary + secondary conversion actions combined. Useful context, never the optimization target.
Cost / conv. (CPA) Cost ÷ conversions. The most important number on the screen for lead gen.
Conv. value / ROAS Revenue ÷ cost. Empty here — itself a finding (no value tracking).
Budget & "Limited by budget" Daily cap. "Limited" = could convert more but hits its cap.
Lost IS (budget) Share of eligible auctions missed for budget. 38% = 4 in 10 chances missed.
Quality Score 1–10 per keyword: expected CTR + ad relevance + landing page. Higher QS = cheaper clicks.
Ad strength Google's RSA asset-diversity rating (Poor→Excellent). Directional, not a KPI.
Broad match Matches loosely related queries. Cheap reach, risky without negatives.
Maximize conversions / tCPA Smart Bidding. Powerful, but re-learns for ~2 weeks after changes.
Performance Max Automated campaigns across Search/Display/YouTube/Gmail/Maps. Runs on assets + audience signals.
Asset group PMax's unit: theme + creatives + audience signal.
Search term vs. keyword Keyword = what you bid on; search term = what the user actually typed.
Auction insights Who else shows on your queries and how often. Competition monitor.
Change history The account audit log. First stop when performance shifts suddenly.
